Life Policy Pilot: Pre-Underwriting Agent
Speciﬁcation

Purpose and status

Proposed product requirement: The AI Customer Health Agent conducts a guided pre-

underwriting interview using the uploaded Detialed-Life-insurance-Questions.pdf as its

controlled source questionnaire. Aer the customer reviews and expressly submits the

responses, the system creates a structured case for Richard Parslow and sends a notiﬁcation to

richardparslow@lifepolicypilot.blog. The uploaded questionnaire covers medical history,

mental health, nicotine, alcohol, avocations, driving, criminal history, bankruptcy, drugs,

residency and travel, occupation and ﬁnances, and military service.[^1]

This document deﬁnes a proposed implementation, not an operating integration. No website,
secure case store, email sender, or mailbox connection has been deployed or veriﬁed. The
destination address is speciﬁed by the business owner; its ownership, security conﬁguration,
and ability to receive notiﬁcations still require veriﬁcation.

The default delivery design should send a secure case notiﬁcation, not expose the completed
medical interview in an ordinary email body. Full-report delivery by email can be supported
through a separately approved secure-message or encrypted-delivery workﬂow. This is a
security design recommendation, not a statement that all health-information email is illegal:
HHS permits email of electronic protected health information when the applicable access,
integrity, and transmission safeguards are satisﬁed.[^2]

Customer interview ﬂow

The proposed workﬂow is introduction → lead form → intake permission → questionnaire →
conditional follow-ups → review and correction → aﬃrmative submission → secure storage →
email notiﬁcation → human review. The customer must be able to ﬁnish an intake without
agreeing to optional marketing calls or texts.

Controlled questioning rules

Use the PDF as a versioned question bank—not merely as a document the model searches from

memory. Each question should have a stable ID, source location, exact original text, approved

customer-facing text, timeframe, answer format, applicability rule, and follow-up links.

Preserve an immutable copy of the approved questionnaire version used in each submitted

case.

The conversation layer may explain unfamiliar words, request clariﬁcation, and make the

interview more approachable. It must not drop exclusions, change “ever” into “recently,” turn a

ﬁve-year question into a seven-year question, or silently substitute a diﬀerent question. Any

proposed change should enter an approval queue before it aﬀects customers.

For long multi-part questions, the interface may collect the components separately, then obtain

conﬁrmation of the complete source question and its combined response. Reusing previously

supplied facts is acceptable only when those facts cover the same scope; the customer should

conﬁrm any proposed answer. Missing information must remain missing, rather than being

converted to “No.”

Recommended answer states are Yes; No; Unknown; Prefer not to answer; Not applicable

under an approved rule; and Not asked. “All applicable questions answered” and “submitted

with unresolved items” must be diﬀerent statuses. A customer who withholds sensitive

information should be oﬀered a human handoﬀ, not coerced into an answer or automatically

rejected.

Supplemental follow-ups may capture diagnosis date, latest treatment, medication details,

hospitalizations, current status, complications, and pending tests when relevant. These

additions must be labeled separately from the source questionnaire. Do not infer a diagnosis

from a medication or represent customer-entered values as independently veriﬁed medical

records.

Required source review

The source includes particularly sensitive questions about HIV/AIDS, mental health and
suicide, substance use, reproductive/sexual health, immigration status, and criminal history; it
also requests a visa or green-card identiﬁcation number in one residency question. It includes
dated terminology and static geographic lists that warrant review before customer-facing
deployment.[^1]

Preserve these items in the internal source inventory, but do not interpret the customer's
instruction to use the PDF as approval to deploy every item unchanged. The recommended
launch gate is documented legal/compliance and underwriting review of sensitive questions,
authorized wording, applicable state/carrier restrictions, and data necessity. If a question
cannot be approved for automated collection, mark it “human review required” with a reason;
do not silently remove it from the coverage audit.

For initial pre-underwriting, the recommended default is not to collect Social Security
numbers, payment details, passport numbers, visa/green-card numbers, identity-document
scans, or unrelated family identiﬁers. If any such information is later necessary, obtain it in an

approved downstream workﬂow rather than casual chat. Height/weight and other useful

underwriting ﬁelds absent from the PDF may be added only as explicitly identiﬁed

supplemental questions.

Because the PDF asks about suicidal thoughts and attempts, distinguish a historical answer

from a disclosure suggesting current crisis. For present crisis, pause the sales interview and

present approved support information; NIMH directs people experiencing suicidal thoughts to

call or text 988, and to call 911 in life-threatening situations. The agent must not claim to

provide emergency monitoring or to have dispatched help.[ 1]

Consent and disclosures

The proposed consent design has separate purposes: permission for the preliminary intake

and submission; acknowledgment of privacy practices; optional marketing-call consent;

optional marketing-text consent; and any later medical-record release. Do not use one

checkbox as a universal authorization for all of these activities.

A short product-level intake acknowledgment could read:

This AI assistant collects information for a preliminary life-insurance review by

Richard Parslow at Life Policy Pilot. It does not diagnose medical conditions or decide

insurance eligibility. Before submission, you can review and correct your answers. By

selecting “Submit for Richard's Review,” you ask Life Policy Pilot to securely provide

your responses to Richard for this review. Submitting this questionnaire does not create

insurance coverage or automatically authorize release of medical records or submission

to an insurer.

This is dra interface language, not a complete legal authorization or substitute for a privacy

notice. Any applicable authorization must identify the actual legal business entity, relevant
information and recipients, purpose, duration, revocation process, and other required
elements. Texas Insurance Code Chapter 602 includes insurance agents and requires speciﬁed
authorization elements where authorization is needed, while also providing exceptions for
necessary insurance functions including underwriting and placement; counsel must assess the
actual workﬂow rather than assume every disclosure always needs a new authorization.[^4]

Maintain the optional, unchecked call and text controls and TCPA disclosure developed for the
lead form. The authorized seller must be the actual legal entity; the disclosure must describe
the methods actually used. Federal written-consent rules require clear authorization for
covered telemarketing technologies, the relevant telephone number, a qualifying signature,
and a disclosure that agreement is not a condition of purchase.[^5]

The customer's instruction to send the submitted case to Richard is not, by itself, permission to
launch automated marketing calls or texts. The product must enforce selected contact
permissions, applicable suppression rules, local-time/state restrictions, and reasonable
revocation requests outside the language model. Permission to provide a requested response

should be recorded separately from ongoing marketing consent, with counsel reviewing the

actual content and technology.[^5]

Email delivery design

Notiﬁcation recipient: richardparslow@lifepolicypilot.blog. Conﬁgure this destination on the

server as an approved recipient. The visitor and language model must not be able to replace it,

add arbitrary recipients, or change mail routing through conversational instructions.

The recommended notiﬁcation template is deliberately minimal:

Subject: New Life Policy Pilot pre-underwriting submission — Case [reference]

A customer has submitted a pre-underwriting questionnaire for your review.

Sign in to the secure review portal to view the submitted answers and case summary.

Case reference: [reference]

Submission time: [timestamp]

Status: Submitted for review

Keep names, birth dates, diagnoses, medications, criminal details, and questionnaire

attachments out of the default notiﬁcation. The portal link should lead to authentication;

possession of a forwarded email alone must not grant case access. Require an authorized

account, preferably with multifactor authentication, and check case access permissions on

every request.

If full-report email delivery is required, use an approved secure-message or encrypted-delivery

service and review mailbox access, forwarding, retention, recovery, and vendor arrangements.

Do not assume a password-protected attachment sent with its password in the same email is
adequate. HHS does not categorically prohibit email of ePHI, but it requires covered entities to

assess the safeguards and document the transmission-security decision.[^2]

Submission should ﬁrst create the case successfully in the secure store, then queue the
notiﬁcation. Record provider acceptance, delivery events where available, failures, and retries
without writing the questionnaire into ordinary email-provider logs. Retries must not create
duplicate cases. When email fails, preserve the case and show an honest notiﬁcation status
rather than claiming successful delivery.

or an underwriting decision. Each summarized fact should link back to the customer's answer.

Carrier-speciﬁc screening, if later added, should be a separate, source-controlled feature

consequential AI decisions and also highlights licensing boundaries.[^6]

Privacy and security checks

Determine legal applicability before making a HIPAA-compliance claim. HHS lists life insurers

among organizations generally outside the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules; covered-entity or
business-associate status depends on the actual role and relationship, not merely on collecting

health answers. Texas also has insurance-speciﬁc health-information protections that expressly
include agents and interact with other state requirements.[ 4]

7][

Regardless of HIPAA applicability, the proposed production requirements are encrypted

transport and storage, restricted administrative access, multifactor authentication,
documented retention/deletion schedules, audit trails, incident procedures, and vendor review.

Exclude health responses from advertising pixels, session-replay tools, ordinary analytics
events, URL query strings, public document links, and unapproved general-purpose model

training. Prevent cross-customer retrieval and remove health-content payloads from routine
application and monitoring logs.

Where HIPAA applies, assess vendors and business associate arrangements as part of the actual

data ﬂow; business associates and their relevant subcontractors have speciﬁc contractual and
safeguard obligations. Security requirements must cover the model provider, hosting,

database, backups, forms/chat service, email service, and every integration that receives the
data—not only the chat widget.[^7]

Production acceptance tests

The following are proposed launch criteria, not proof that a system has already passed them.

1. Question coverage: Reconcile every PDF question against the approved bank; document

sensitive-item restrictions and every intentional revision.

2. Branch coverage: Verify that applicable follow-ups are asked, irrelevant conditional

questions are skipped, and repeated timeframes/exclusions remain distinct.

3. Answer integrity: Conﬁrm that blanks, uncertainty, and refusal are never saved as “No,”

and that the bot does not invent medical facts.

4. Review and submission: Prove that the customer can correct answers, changes invalidate

the prior review conﬁrmation, and no case notiﬁcation is sent without aﬃrmative
submission.

5. Consent separation: Completing the questionnaire with marketing boxes unchecked

creates no marketing enrollment; selected permissions and later withdrawals propagate
correctly.

6. Recipient control: Prove that prompt injection and client-side changes cannot alter the

approved email recipient or expose another case.

7. Secure access: Verify that a forwarded notiﬁcation does not provide access without

authentication and authorization.

8. Delivery reliability: Test secure-store failure, notiﬁcation failure, retry handling, duplicate

prevention, and accurate conﬁrmation wording.

9. Leak prevention: Check analytics, logs, error reports, email bodies, and browser URLs for

sensitive-data exposure.

10. Human review: Verify that consequential recommendations and any carrier-facing actions

remain behind an authorized human approval step.

Launch dependencies

Before implementation is treated as production-ready, conﬁrm the actual legal operating entity,

licensed service states, approved questionnaire version, sensitive-question rules, privacy
notice, authorization forms, TCPA disclosure, hosting/model vendors, secure intake store,

authenticated review portal, retention schedule, and email-provider conﬁguration. Verify
access to the speciﬁed recipient mailbox without sharing its password in chat.

An end-to-end test should use clearly labeled non-customer test inputs to conﬁrm that an

interview can be reviewed, submitted, securely stored, and notiﬁed to the intended mailbox. A
written speciﬁcation or system prompt alone cannot provide these backend controls, establish

legal compliance, or send real submissions.
